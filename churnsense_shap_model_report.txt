MODEL SELECTION SUMMARY

Three models were trained and compared on the same dataset and evaluation set. Missed churners are counted on the held-out test set.

| Model               | AUC-ROC | Recall | Precision | F1    | Missed Churners (test set) |
|---------------------|---------|--------|-----------|-------|-----------------|
| Logistic Regression | 0.900   | 0.940  | 0.520     | 0.670 | 235             |
| XGBoost             | 0.892   | 0.852  | 0.585     | 0.694 | 308             |
| Random Forest       | 0.895   | 0.621  | 0.698     | 0.658 | 788             |

SELECTED MODEL: Logistic Regression

Reasons for selection:
1. Highest recall (0.940) — catches 94% of actual churners, the business priority
2. Fewest missed churners on the test set (235 vs 788 for Random Forest) — directly translates to retained revenue
3. Fully interpretable coefficients — can explain every prediction to a non-technical stakeholder
4. Lower computational cost — suitable for real-time scoring in a production API
5. Consistent with SHAP explainability requirements

Decision threshold was tuned from default 0.50 to 0.35 to maximise recall at the cost of some precision. This is appropriate for a retention use case where false negatives (missing a churner) are far more costly than false positives (offering retention to a non-churner).

---

MODEL PERFORMANCE METRICS

Final model (Logistic Regression, threshold = 0.35):
- AUC-ROC: 0.900 — excellent discriminative ability
- Recall: 0.940 — catches 94 out of every 100 actual churners
- Precision: 0.520 — 52% of flagged customers are genuine churners
- F1 Score: 0.670 — harmonic mean of precision and recall
- Decision Threshold: 0.35 (optimised for recall)

Confusion matrix interpretation at threshold 0.35:
- True Positives: customers correctly identified as churners — the primary target
- False Negatives: churners missed by the model — minimised by lowering threshold
- False Positives: non-churners flagged as at-risk — acceptable cost in retention context
- True Negatives: non-churners correctly identified as safe

AUC-ROC of 0.900 means the model has a 90% probability of correctly ranking a churner above a non-churner — strong performance for a binary classification task on real-world data.

SHAP ANALYSIS — GLOBAL FEATURE IMPORTANCE

SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) was used to explain model predictions at both global and individual level. SHAP values measure each feature's contribution to the prediction relative to the base rate.

TOP 6 FEATURES BY MEAN ABSOLUTE SHAP VALUE:

Rank 1 — Contract Type Encoded
- Mean |SHAP|: 2.09
- Direction: Month-to-Month contracts push churn probability strongly upward
- Insight: Contract type is by far the most important predictor. A Month-to-Month customer has a SHAP contribution of approximately +2.09 to the log-odds of churning compared to an annual customer.
- Business implication: Converting a customer from Month-to-Month to Annual is the single most impactful retention action available.

Rank 2 — Customer Tenure Months
- Mean |SHAP|: 0.99
- Direction: Short tenure pushes churn probability upward; long tenure pushes it downward
- Insight: Every additional month of tenure reduces churn risk. Customers in their first 6 months have tenure SHAP values that can exceed +1.5.
- Business implication: The first 6 months are the critical retention window. Early onboarding investment has the highest ROI.

Rank 3 — Satisfaction Score
- Mean |SHAP|: 0.41
- Direction: Low satisfaction scores push churn probability upward
- Insight: Moderate predictive power. Signal is weaker than expected, likely due to data quality issues (high missing rate, imputation may have diluted signal).
- Business implication: Satisfaction scores should be collected more systematically to improve this feature's signal in model v2.

Rank 4 — Number of Support Calls (Last 6 Months)
- Mean |SHAP|: 0.33
- Direction: More support calls push churn probability upward
- Insight: 3 or more support calls in 6 months is a strong churn signal. Each additional call beyond 3 increases churn probability meaningfully.
- Business implication: Customers with 3+ support calls should be automatically escalated to the retention team for proactive outreach.

Rank 5 — Return Rate
- Mean |SHAP|: 0.18
- Direction: High return rates push churn probability upward
- Insight: Frequent returns indicate product dissatisfaction or mismatched expectations — a form of friction that precedes churn.
- Business implication: High-return customers should receive product education or be offered alternative products to reduce friction.

Rank 6 — NPS Score
- Mean |SHAP|: 0.16
- Direction: Low NPS pushes churn probability upward
- Insight: NPS is a weaker signal than support calls despite its reputation as a churn predictor. May improve with better data collection.
- Business implication: Monitor NPS trends but do not use it as the sole trigger for retention outreach.

LOW-IMPORTANCE FEATURES (near-zero SHAP):
- Gender: SHAP value < 0.01 — no predictive power, flagged for removal in v2
- Region (North/South/East/West): SHAP value < 0.01 — no predictive power, flagged for removal in v2
- Payment method: SHAP value < 0.05 — minimal predictive power

INDIVIDUAL PREDICTION EXPLANATION

The ChurnSense API returns a waterfall-style explanation for every individual prediction. Example output for a high-risk customer:

Customer profile:
- Contract: Month-to-Month
- Tenure: 3 months
- Support calls (6m): 5
- Satisfaction score: 2.5/10
- NPS: 4/10
- Return rate: 12%

Prediction output:
- Churn probability: 87%
- Risk label: High Risk
- Top risk factors:
  1. contract_type_encoded: SHAP +1.73 (Month-to-Month contract)
  2. customer_tenure_months: SHAP +1.04 (very short tenure, 3 months)
  3. num_support_calls_last_6m: SHAP +0.62 (5 calls — high frustration signal)
  4. satisfaction_score: SHAP +0.38 (low satisfaction at 2.5)

Interpretation: "This customer is at 87% churn risk. The primary driver is their Month-to-Month contract combined with very short tenure (3 months). Five support calls in 6 months indicate active frustration. Recommended action: contact within 48 hours, offer Annual contract upgrade incentive."

---

SHAP DEPENDENCE PLOTS — KEY RELATIONSHIPS

Contract Type vs Tenure interaction:
- New Month-to-Month customers (under 6 months) have the highest combined SHAP values
- The interaction between contract type and tenure is the most powerful churn signal in the model
- Annual contract customers show negative SHAP values regardless of tenure — they are stable

Support Calls dependence:
- SHAP values for support calls increase non-linearly
- 1–2 calls: modest increase in churn risk
- 3 calls: significant jump in churn risk
- 5+ calls: near-maximum contribution to churn probability
- Threshold of 3 calls is the operational trigger point for retention escalation

FEATURE CORRELATION WITH CHURN (EDA confirmation)

- contract_type → churn: strong positive correlation (Month-to-Month = high churn)
- customer_tenure_months → churn: strong negative correlation (longer = lower churn)
- num_support_calls_last_6m → churn: moderate positive correlation
- satisfaction_score → churn: moderate negative correlation (lower score = higher churn)
- monthly_spend → churn: near-zero correlation (spend does not predict churn)
- region → churn: near-zero correlation (geography does not predict churn)
- gender → churn: near-zero correlation (gender does not predict churn)
